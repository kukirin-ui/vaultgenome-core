## 1. Scope and what changed since the last model

The threat model of 2026-05-06 described a control plane and four TEE
backends (AWS Nitro, Azure SGX, GCP SEV-SNP, Intel SGX bare metal). Since
then the shipped surface moved:

- **Backends.** The product attests and verifies on `gcp-sev-snp`,
  `gcp-tdx` (ADR 0018, 2026-09-16) and `azure-cgpu` (ADR 0019, 0021: the
  chip, the vTPM and the H100 in one evidence, with the verifier's own
  evaluation of the GPU's report and NVIDIA's revocation service). AWS
  Nitro and SGX are measurement kits under `scripts/hardware-test/`, not
  shipped verifiers (`VERIFIABLE-CLAIMS.md`, "What we do not claim").
- **Control plane.** The Return Path with every decision on an append-only
  record before it takes effect (ADR 0014); the sentinel and policy-driven
  failover with one serial, one move and the operator's stop list (ADR
  0012, 0017); the escrow key sealed to the release host's TEE or vTPM
  (ADR 0016, 0022); every daemon key file sealed to the host (ADR 0023);
  recorded refusals (ADR 0010); the integer door for the worker (ADR 0020).
- **Supply chain.** Go 1.27.2, every action and image pinned, hash-locked
  Python runtime, keyless release signatures with SLSA provenance, signed
  tags with the signers in git, a rehearsal tag before every release
  (0.4.0, 2026-10-14).

The model's sections for the shipped backends did not exist; the review's
companion change adds them and marks the kit-only backends as such.

## 3. Residual risks

Unchanged from the threat model's cross-platform table: the TEE vendors'
silicon and firmware, side channels not yet public, root rotations during
a long session, the insider, the supply chain beyond what pins and
reproducibility can show, and quantum cryptanalysis. Two are sharpened by
the shipped backends: on `azure-cgpu` the NVIDIA local verifier and
`tpm2-tools` are operator-installed processes the daemon runs, so their
integrity is the host's (the OS and driver are in the vTPM's PCRs, the
GPU's firmware in NVIDIA's verdict); and NVIDIA's reference manifests are
fetched from NVIDIA's service, verified to a root pinned in the binary,
then cached — a stale cache is a stale reference until the next refresh.
